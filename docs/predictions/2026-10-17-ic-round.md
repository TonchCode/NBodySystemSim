# IC-round predictions (cuda-architect), 2026-10-17: the IC workload suite

**Status: pre-registration.** CLAUDE.md rule 7: this file must be committed *before* the phase-2
timing and validation runs that test it (separate commit). Design: `docs/03-design.md` §19.
Decisions: D-027 (normalised tree gates), D-028 (stack capacity), D-029 (ICs, mass spectrum, viewer).
Code: `src/core/init.cpp`, `src/core/kernels_tree.cu`, `src/bench/main.cpp`, `src/viewer/main.cpp`.
Tags: [M] measured · [D] derived · [P:key] paper. θ is always the bmax-θ (§11.4).

**Scoring rule** (unchanged from tree round 2, design §17.8). Every item has an ID. A range item is a
HIT if the measured value (median of the runs for times; the single deterministic value for counts
and errors) lies in [lo, hi], a MISS otherwise. A directional item is a HIT if the direction holds.
**One ID = one scorecard row**; no item is split or reinterpreted after this commit; a disputed
reading is listed as disputed. **(A)** = anchored on a §0 observation (flagged in the scorecard).
**(C)** = holds by construction, listed as a check. Phase 2 writes
`results/2026-10-17_ic_round/scorecard.csv` (id, predicted, measured, source log, HIT/MISS, anchored).

**Why this round.** The owner asked for bodies in shapes (a galaxy, a cube outline), live N in the
viewer and heavier bodies. Each IC is a different *imbalance generator* for the tree walks: spatial
distribution sets the per-body cost distribution (intra-warp imbalance, what #5 and #7 attack) and
the per-region cost distribution (between-warp and SM-level imbalance, what #6 attacks). So every IC
is measured with #4, #5, #6 (warp queue) and #7; none is visual-only.

- **Q5** (the question asked): is there an IC where #6 finally wins (ratio > 1.00)? **Predicted:
  no.** Round 2 showed the warp queue loses intra-block L1 locality (−6 to −10%) and gains only the
  SM-level loss, which is 1–2% at N ≥ 262144. At 65536 every warp gets at most one unit (N < 122,880
  bodies), so the queue only re-assigns units to SMs. The one regime where it could win is a
  single-wave launch whose expensive units cluster in a short Morton range: galaxies at 65536. Even
  there the expected SM-level loss (tail 15–30%) is mostly recovered by the hardware scheduler's
  block order, which already spreads consecutive block indices over SMs. P(Q5 = yes) ≤ 15%.
- **Q6**: on galaxies at 65536 the warp-unit timeline shows #6's SM efficiency ≥ #5's (the
  balance metric improves) while Q1 says the time does not: the round-2 pattern repeats.

## 8. Mass spectrum (plummer and disk, 262144, θ = 0.5, protocol default leaf 16 b128)

| ID | item | prediction |
|---|---|---|
| K1 | #5 work: plummer salpeter / plummer equal | 1.08–1.22 (A: 1.15 at 8192) |
| K2 | #5 work: plummer heavy-few / plummer equal | 0.98–1.04 (A) |
| K3 | #7 / #5 traversal speedup, salpeter vs equal plummer | within ±12% |
| K4 | normalised rms, salpeter and heavy-few vs equal plummer (#5 and #7, 4 rows) | ×[0.85, 1.20] |
| K5 | heavy bodies' normalised max ≤ light bodies' p99 × 2 (salpeter, heavy-few, disk) | holds |
| K6 | disk salpeter vs disk equal: #5 work ratio | 1.05–1.25 |

- Why K1: the bmax criterion is purely geometric, but bmax is measured from the **COM**: with a
  spread of masses the COM of a small cell is pulled towards its heaviest body, often off-centre,
  so bmax (COM to the farthest corner) grows and the cell is opened at a larger distance. Small
  cells near the leaves are affected, large cells average out. Heavy-few touches only the ~16 ×
  depth ancestors of the heavy bodies.
- Why K4/K5 (error does *not* grow): a cell with one dominant body has its COM near that body, so its
  quadrupole is small relative to M·bmax²; and the larger bmax makes the criterion more
  conservative. The cell accepted "like any other" is therefore no worse, as §0.6 already suggests
  at 8192.
- **Softening and dt** [D, D-029]: ε = 0.01 is a global Plummer softening; it does not change with
  mass. The softened peak acceleration near a body of mass m is (2/3^1.5) G m / ε², so the
  dynamical time there is √(ε/a). With η = 0.1 (a convention, TODO(source)), dt ≤ 0.1 √(ε³ / (0.385 G
  m_max)): 1.6e-3 for the disk's central body (m = 0.01) and for heavy-few's 1% bodies, 2.6e-3 for
  salpeter at 8192 (m_max ∝ 1/N, so looser at large N). The bench and the viewer print this
  advisory; the viewer's default dt = 2e-3 is above it for disk/galaxies/heavy-few (orbits within a
  few ε of the heaviest body are not converged; a visual, not a measurement). The force-snapshot
  measurements of this round do not integrate, so dt does not affect them.

## 9. Tree depth, stack and the cube_shell question

| ID | item | prediction |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | push depth at 1M (`tree:` line), every new IC | ≤ 45; stack bound ≤ 47 < 64: 0 overflows in every run |
| D2 | cube_edges at 1M: duplicate keys | 1.5–2.6% of bodies (expected 21.8k pairs [D]: n_e²/(2·2²¹) per edge, n_e = N/12) |

**Where `cube_shell` breaks things (C1–C4):**
- **C1:** it does *not* break the tree: it is the shallowest structured IC (depth 25 at 65536, §0.5),
  with no duplicate keys (D3), flat AABBs are legal, and the exact host check passes (§0.4).
- **C2:** it breaks the **legacy per-body relative gate** (E5): #5's rms rel error exceeds 0.03.
- **C3:** it has the **second-largest union overhead** of all ICs (×2.0–2.4 in interactions at every
  N; only uniform is higher), because a 32-body patch of a sheet is wide relative to the opening
  distances of its flat cells.
- **C4:** `cube_edges` is the real stress case: duplicate keys (D2), the deepest new tree (D4), the
  largest #4 intra-warp imbalance (M4), the legacy gate failure, and the only IC where TG3's premise
  (a finer walk is at least as accurate in aggregate) fails, for the random-order union walk (§0.4).
