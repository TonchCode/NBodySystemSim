## 0. Already known before phase 2 (not predictions)

Observed in phase 1. Evidence in `results/2026-10-17_round2/phase1_checks/` and
`results/2026-10-17_round2/probe/`.
1. **Production SASS unchanged** [M]. All 57 production kernels (tiled 2, tuned 25,
   multi 30) are instruction-for-instruction identical to round 1 (`tools/sass_diff.py`).
   The instrumented (TL) twins differ by −3…+4 registers in some configs, mostly absorbed by
   the 256-register allocation granularity. None of the configs measured in §1 changes register count or occupancy,
   except multi b1024 (55 → 57 regs: still 1 block/SM, thread-limited).
2. **`%globaltimer` ticks in 1.024 µs steps** on this board [M]. That is too coarse for
   10–30 µs kernels. Fix: a calibration kernel before and after the timeline launches anchors
   each SM's `clock64` to a globaltimer tick edge. All block times are clock64-based and
   placed on the global axis (`tools/timeline_analysis.py`). In the one functional run, every
   calibrated start agreed with its raw stamp to within one tick, and the per-SM clock was
   2.6065–2.6095 GHz.
3. **One functional timeline run, N = 2048** (multi b256 q4, 32 blocks, 2 runs, no warm-up):
   32 active SMs with 1 block each, active max/mean 1.006, tail 1.0% of span. This bears on
   P-TL1 below (B ≤ 80 → one block per SM). I saw it before writing P-TL1.
4. **Self-test** [M]. `--selftest` PASS: 1810 cases (every variant × 10 N × timeline on/off).
   Injected bugs (tiled drops the last body; multi b512 q32 pf1 drops one j per tile) fail
   exactly those configs. `--selftest-drop J` fails every case where the drop is visible.
5. **Probe SASS** [M, static counts per interaction, full-unroll b128]: base 17.38 slots;
   ftz 14.38 (−3: FSETP + 2 FMUL); mufu2 19.38 (**+2**: MUFU + FSEL); fmul3 20.38 (+3:
   FFMA + 2 FMUL). All four use 40 regs and 0 spills. The pure-issue model therefore predicts
   ftz −17.3%, mufu2 **+11.5%** (the round-1 summary figure, which is correct; the probe header's
   +6% assumed +1 slot), fmul3 +17.3%. Round-1 measurements: −10%, +19%, +18.5%.
6. **Provenance re-computation from round-1 data.** `tools/rate_curve.py` reproduces the
   per-SM curve: 2.0 G/s/warp at 2 warps, 2.1 at 4, 13.7 G/s at 8, 17.0 at 32. There is
   **no 6-warp point** in the data, so the round-1 "linear up to ~6 warps" was an
   interpolation. `tools/autoq_models.py` reproduces v1 (1.8% / 0.9%) and the saturation rule
   (6.8% / 4.8%) exactly. **The reconstructed v2 gives 1.0% in-sample / 0.6% out-of-sample, i.e.
   better than v1 out-of-sample.** That contradicts the D-008 rejection reason. Either the lost
   round-1 v2 differed from this reconstruction, or the round-1 claim was wrong. I can't tell
   which, so the claim is withdrawn (to be logged as D-010). The difference is 0.3 percentage
   points on 12 cells, too small to choose either model.

## 1. Load imbalance (finding #1), timeline metrics

Protocol (fixed now): `tools/capture_env.ps1` before/after. For each config:
`nbody_bench --kernel K --n N --block B [--unroll U | --q Q --prefetch 1] --warmup-ms 300
--runs 21 --batch {50 if N ≤ 10000, else 5} --timeline tl.csv --timeline-launches 5`,
then `tools/timeline_analysis.py` (median over the 5 launches).
Configs per N ∈ {1000, 4096, 10000, 65536}: **tiled b256** (default), **tuned b128 full**
(best 1-thread/body config in round 1 at every N), **multi best** (round 1: N=1000 b512 q32;
4096 b512 q8; 10000 b1024 q8; 65536 b256 q4).

Definitions (tool): max/mean = max SM busy / mean SM busy over **all 80 SMs** (idle = 0).
active = the same over active SMs only. tail = last block end − median per-SM last end,
as % of span.

**P-TL1.** For every config with B ≤ 80 blocks, each active SM gets **exactly one block**
(blocks/SM max = 1). Then idle = 80 − B and max/mean = (80/B) × active, with active in
[1.00, 1.03].

| N | config | B | idle SMs | max/mean (80 SMs) | active | tail % | blocks/SM |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1000 | tiled b256 | 4 | 76 | 20.0 (19.4–20.6) | ≤ 1.03 | ≤ 5 | 1 |
| 1000 | tuned b128 full | 8 | 72 | 10.0 (9.7–10.3) | ≤ 1.03 | ≤ 5 | 1 |
| 1000 | multi b512 q32 | 63 | 17 | 1.27 (1.23–1.31) | ≤ 1.03 | ≤ 10 | 1 |
| 4096 | **tiled b256** | **16** | **64** | **5.0 (4.85–5.15)** | ≤ 1.03 | **≤ 3** | 1 |
| 4096 | tuned b128 full | 32 | 48 | 2.5 (2.43–2.58) | ≤ 1.03 | ≤ 3 | 1 |
| 4096 | **multi b512 q8** | **64** | **16** | **1.25 (1.21–1.29)** | ≤ 1.03 | **≤ 3** | 1 |
| 10000 | tiled b256 | 40 | 40 | 2.0 (1.94–2.06) | ≤ 1.03 | ≤ 3 | 1 |
| 10000 | tuned b128 full | 79 | 1 | 1.013 (1.00–1.04) | ≤ 1.03 | ≤ 3 | 1 |
| 10000 | multi b1024 q8 | 79 | 1 | 1.013 (1.00–1.04) | ≤ 1.03 | ≤ 3 | 1 |
| 65536 | **tiled b256** | **256** | **0** | **1.20 (1.12–1.26)** | = same | **20 (13–27)** | **3..4** |
| 65536 | tuned b128 full | 512 | 0 | 1.09 (1.04–1.11) | = same | 12 (6–16) | 6..7 |
| 65536 | **multi b256 q4** | **1024** | **0** | **1.04 (1.00–1.08)** | = same | **5 (2–10)** | 12..14 |

Reasoning [D]:
- *N = 65536 tiled b256:* all 256 blocks fit at once (6/SM × 80 = 480 slots). With
  breadth-first placement, 16 SMs get 4 blocks and 64 get 3. The per-SM rate is near saturation
  at 24–32 warps (16.5–17 G/s), so busy time ∝ blocks: max/mean = 4/3.2 = 1.25. That drops toward
  1.15 if the 3-block SMs run slower per SM (fewer warps). The tail is the 4th block's
  share, (4 − 3)/4 = 25% minus the rate effect. SM efficiency (mean busy / span) 0.80
  (0.78–0.88). Round-1 tiled b256 1089 G/s ⇒ 3.94 ms. That matches the busiest SM's
  4 × 256 × 65536 / 17 G/s = 3.95 ms, which is why I hold this row with the most confidence.
- *tuned b128 full at 65536:* 512 blocks, 12/SM resident. 32 SMs get 7, 48 get 6:
  7/6.4 = 1.094, tail (7 − 6)/7 = 14%.
- *multi b256 q4 at 65536:* 1024 blocks > 480 slots, so dynamic dispatch. Each SM takes the next
  block when one finishes, and the end is ragged by at most about one block's run time at
  falling concurrency. That is the mechanism by which q fixes wave quantization here.
- *B ≤ 80 rows:* all blocks start at once (start skew predicted ≤ 1 µs) and do identical
  work. The imbalance is pure starvation: 80 − B SMs never get work.

**P-TL2 (SM clock):** the calibrated SM clock during the timeline launches is 2.70–2.80 GHz
for every config at N ≥ 4096 (round 1: 2760 MHz median under load), the same within 1%
across configs at one N.

**P-TL3 (what the timeline will show about #2 vs #3):** at N = 4096 the R3 #3 gain is
**starvation removal**. Active SMs go 16 → 64 for tiled b256 → multi b512 q8, and 32 → 64 for
tuned b128 full → multi. Per-active-SM busy time stays balanced (≤ 1.03) in all three. At
N = 65536 the R3 #2/#3 gains are **tail removal**: tail 20% (tiled b256) → 12% (tuned b128) →
5% (multi q4).

## 2. Prefetch vs q (finding #4)

Protocol: N = 4096 and 65536, `--warmup-ms 300 --runs 21 --batch {50 | 5}`. For each block B:
`tuned u8` (control), `multi q1 pf0`, `multi q1 pf1`, `multi q_best pf0`, `multi q_best pf1`.
N = 4096: B ∈ {64, 128, 256, 512} and q_best = {8, 16, 8, 8} (round-1 best q per block). N = 65536: B = 256, q ∈ {1, 4}.

**P-PF0 (control):** multi q1 pf0 = tuned u8 at the same block, within ±3% (same loop, same
unroll 8; only the 2D indexing differs).

**P-PF1 (prefetch at q = 1, N = 4096):** the gain falls with warps per SM (the exposed L2
load per tile is hidden only by other warps):

| block (warps/SM) | tuned u8 r1 | multi q1 pf1 r1 | predicted pf1 / pf0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 64 (2) | 258 | 304 | **+18% (+12…+22)** |
| 128 (4) | 274 | 302 | **+10% (+6…+14)** |
| 256 (8) | 223 | 232 | +4% (0…+8) |
| 512 (16) | 132 | 138 | +4% (0…+8) |

**P-PF2 (prefetch at the best q):** multi b512 q8: pf1 / pf0 = **+7% (+4…+12)**. Each
16-warp SM computes ≈ 2 µs per tile, against ≈ 0.1–0.2 µs of exposed L2 load + barrier without
prefetch [D].

**P-PF3 (decomposition of the N = 4096 gain over the best-tuned baseline, rule 8):** baseline
= tuned b128 full, 349 G/s (round 1). multi b512 q8 pf1 = 893 (2.56×). Prediction:
multi b512 q8 **pf0 ≈ 835 (780–870)**. So **q accounts for ≈ 2.39× (2.25–2.50) and prefetch
for ≈ 1.07× (1.04–1.12)**. Prefetch is ≈ 7% of the log-gain (4–12%). The critic's concern
(a large part of #3's gain is prefetch) is **predicted false at the best config** and **true
at 2–4 warps/SM** (b64/b128 q1). Caveat, stated now: multi runs unroll 8 while the baseline
is full-unroll. tuned b128 is +27% from u8 → full at N = 4096, so a third factor (unroll)
is confounded in the *other* direction. It is noted, not measured here.

**P-PF4 (N = 65536):** b256 q1 pf1 / pf0 = +3% (0…+5); b256 q4 pf1 / pf0 = +2% (0…+5).
24–48 warps/SM hide the load.

## 4. Long-run energy (finding #10)

Protocol: N = 4096, Plummer seed 42, eps = 0.01, KDK. Each step is 3 separate launches, each
well under 2 s. FP64 host energy every K steps:
- E1: tiled b256, dt = 1e-3, 100,000 steps (**100 time units**), K = 1000
- E2: tiled b256, dt = 5e-4, 200,000 steps (100 time units), K = 2000
- E3: multi b512 q8, dt = 1e-3, 100,000 steps, K = 1000
`--steps S --dt dt --energy-log e.csv --energy-every K`.

Round 1: −2.76e-8 at t = 2. Predictions [D, order-of-magnitude]:
- **P-E1:** |ΔE/E| at t = 100 for E1 in **[1e-7, 3e-6]**, central 5e-7. The log-log slope of
  the |ΔE/E| envelope over t ∈ [1, 100] is α ∈ [0.4, 1.1]: random-walk-like (0.5) to secular (1).
- **P-E2 (what dominates):** FP32 round-off, not leapfrog truncation. Each step perturbs x and v
  by ~u·|x|, u·|v| with random sign, which adds a random walk in E. Truncation error of
  KDK is O(dt²) and mostly bounded. Prediction: |ΔE/E|(E2) / |ΔE/E|(E1) at t = 100 in
  **[0.7, 2.0]**. Truncation-dominated would give ≈ 0.25; round-off-dominated gives ≈ √2.
- **P-E3:** multi vs tiled: same magnitude, |ΔE/E|(E3) / |ΔE/E|(E1) in [0.3, 3]. Neither is
  systematically better. Trajectories diverge chaotically within a few time units, so the
  curves are not comparable point by point. The round-1 1e-9 difference was noise.
- Plummer is in virial equilibrium. Over 100 time units (≈ 0.6 t_relax at N = 4096 [D]) the
  virial ratio stays within ±5% of 1. The logged KE/PE checks this.

## 5. Gates (D-009) and validation

- **P-G1:** every config in the round-2 sweep (tiled, tuned 25, multi 30 × pf {0, 1}) at
  N ∈ {1000, 4096, 10000, 65536} passes G0, G1 and G2. q = 1 orders (tiled, tuned, multi q1) sit
  at normMax / (u√N) ≈ 0.45–0.70, a 1.4–2.2× margin to G1. multi q ≥ 2 sits lower (≈ 1/q-ish,
  as in round 1).
- **P-G2:** with largest-κ selection, κ_max of the FP64 sample rises with N: 30–300 at
  N = 4096 and **100–3000 at N = 65536** (round 1, smallest-|a| selection: κ_max ≈ 15). Naive's
  normMax at 65536 stays within ±30% of round 1 (7.1e-6), because normMax does not see κ.
  rms-rel and max-rel *do* rise with the new sample. That is the D-009 point.
- `--selftest` stays PASS (already observed, §0.4; re-run as part of the session).

## 6. Probe re-run (finding #2), `tools/run_probe.ps1` with the 100 ms clock / power log

- **P-PR1:** the ratios reproduce round 1 within ±2 percentage points: ftz 0.88–0.92,
  mufu2 1.17–1.21, fmul3 1.16–1.21.
- **P-PR2:** the median SM clock per variant is within ±1% of base. So the round-1 misfits
  (mufu2 +19% vs +11.5% issue model; ftz −10% vs −17.3%) are **not** clock artifacts. If
  they are clock artifacts (> 1% clock difference), the clock-corrected ratios are the ones
  scored. Board power during the probe is 230–272 W; it may touch the 272 W cap.
- Interpretation registered now: if P-PR2 holds, mufu2's excess (+7.5 pp over issue) is the XU
  co-limit (XU ≈ 8/19.4 = 83% busy in mufu2 vs 46% in base [D]). ftz's shortfall means
  that once the guard is removed, something other than issue limits the base kernel: latency
  or dependency stalls, or register-bank conflicts. Only ncu can tell those apart. The
  wording for 03-design stays "issue-limited with a significant XU co-limit".

## Post-registration note (2026-10-17, after the phase-2 runs; nothing above was edited)

1. **Scoring is in the round-2 results report** (scorecard: 25 rows, 18 HIT, 7 MISS).
2. **Exploratory run, not part of the pre-registration:** tuned b64/b256 timelines at
   N = 65536 (`results/2026-10-17_round2/timeline_exploratory/`). They were run after scoring, to locate
   the round-1 P2a miss. They are not scored.
3. **P-PR2 resolution:** nvidia-smi refreshes clocks about every 0.5 s and the probe's four
   variants run about 0.35 s each, so per-variant clocks can't be resolved. Only the whole-run band
   (2610–2640 MHz) is available. The first probe run's clock log was empty (tool bug, fixed in
   `tools/run_probe.ps1`); the probe was repeated, and both runs' outputs are kept.
4. **Tail metric:** "tail" is measured from the median SM's last block end (as defined in §1).
   When most SMs carry the maximum load it goes to ~0 even with a quantized load (e.g. 64 SMs
   with 13 blocks, 16 with 12). It is meant to be read with max/mean. The definition was not
   changed after the fact.
