Requirement: where it is used and why it is useful. The idea, "summarise far-away groups by one number", turns O(N²) into about O(N log N) wherever a force falls off with distance. Cosmology: GADGET-4 (Springel et al. 2021, tree plus fast multipole), PKDGRAV3 (Potter et al. 2017, trillion-particle simulations for the Euclid satellite on 4,000+ GPU nodes). Planets: GENGA (Grimm & Stadel 2014, GPU, up to 30× faster than the CPU code Mercury), REBOUND (Rein & Liu 2012, open source, widely used). Outside physics: Barnes-Hut t-SNE (van der Maaten 2014); ForceAtlas2, Gephi's default layout (Jacomy et al. 2014); d3-force, the JavaScript library that lays out graphs in web pages, builds a quadtree and uses Barnes-Hut with θ = 0.9 by default. A web page drawing a network graph runs the same algorithm as our simulation, in 2D, with a looser θ. Why a GPU: in one step every body's force is independent of every other's, which is exactly what thousands of FP32 lanes are for.
