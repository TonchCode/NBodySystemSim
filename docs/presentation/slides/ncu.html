Nsight Compute reads the GPU's hardware counters per kernel. It was blocked for most of the project (performance-counter permission); it was run at the end from an administrator prompt. Diagnostic run, not pre-registered; the times under the profiler are not bench times. How to read the stall row: "memory" is long_scoreboard, a warp waiting for a load from L1/L2. "none: ready" is not_selected: the warp was ready but the scheduler picked another one, which means the SM is busy, which is good. All-pairs: two-thirds of issue slots used, DRAM and L2 idle: issue-bound, as claimed. The FMA pipe is at 69 % and the rsqrt (XU) pipe at 39 %, the SFU co-limit. Its achieved occupancy is only 39 %: 256 blocks for 80 SMs, the starvation from slide 10, seen by the hardware. #4 and #5: the warps mostly wait for memory. L1 serves only half the tree loads; the misses go to L2, which is at 95 % of its throughput, while DRAM is idle (L2 hit rate 99.7 %). So "L1-capacity bound" means L1 misses saturate the L1-to-L2 path, and 39 % of that traffic is the per-thread stack. #7: no local memory, 14× fewer sectors than #5 (25× per interaction), L2 down to 24 %, issue slots 69 % busy. The tree kernel became compute-bound. One correction: we had called #7 latency-bound. At full occupancy the counters show it near balance between the latency of its pop chain and instruction issue (ALU pipe 62 %). The next step is the same either way: wider tree nodes, fewer pops.
